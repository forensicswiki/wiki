    Chain of custody

    In the digital world, evidence is inherently fragile. Unlike physical evidence such as a fingerprint or a shell casing, digital data can be altered, overwritten, or deleted in a fraction of a second with a single keystroke. To ensure that digital evidence remains legally admissible in a court of law, forensic examiners rely on a foundational process known as the Chain of Custody.

    The Chain of Custody

    The chain of custody is the continuous, contemporaneous, and unbroken chronological documentation that tracks potential digital evidence through its entire lifecycle. This includes its identification, seizure, collection, transfer, analysis, storage, and final disposition. Its primary purpose is to verify accountability and prevent claims of unauthorized alteration, tampering, or spoliation.

    International Standards and Specifications

    To maintain global consistency, standards bodies like ISO/IEC 27037 define the chain of custody as a fundamental tracking mechanism for physical and logical access to evidence. According to these specifications, a compliant chain of custody log must record:

    • Unique Identification: Specific identification numbers assigned to every physical item.
    • Hardware Details: The make, model, serial number, and observed physical condition of the device.
    • Initial Device State: The exact power state of the device upon discovery (powered ON or OFF).
    • Precise Timestamps: Every action and transfer must be logged using 24-hour notation synchronized to Universal Coordinated Time (UTC).
    • Personnel Details: The full identity, title, and contact information of both the releasing and receiving parties.
    • Storage Specifics: Secure storage location details, including physical locker numbers and electronic access logs.

    Organizations like the Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence (SWGDE) reinforce these practices by mandating that any custody transfer, internal lab movement, or external dispatch be logged instantly within formal case notes.

    The Legal Necessity

    In judicial proceedings, an unverified or broken chain of custody can lead to the immediate exclusion of digital evidence. Courts view these logs as the primary defense against claims of evidence tampering.

    • In the US: Under Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE 902(11), 902(13), and 902(14)), chain of custody forms are part of the sworn written certification required to self-authenticate electronic records.
    • In the UK: Under the ACPO Principle 3, the chain of custody forms an integral part of the audit trail, allowing an independent expert witness to track and replicate every operational stage from the crime scene to judicial review.

    Paper Logs versus Cryptographic Hashes

    A crucial analytical distinction exists between chain of custody and data integrity.

    • Cryptographic Hashing proves mathematically that the data has not changed since the copy was made. However, it cannot prove who physically handled the device before it was hashed, or if the correct device was grabbed in the first place.
    • Physical Chain of Custody proves who held the device and where it was stored. However, it cannot rule out technical data corruption caused by a faulty write-blocker or hardware failure. True legal defensibility requires both mechanisms simultaneously: physical tracking logs to account for human handling, and mathematical verification algorithms to prove data immutability.

    Summary

    To withstand legal scrutiny, a forensic investigator must be able to clearly describe how the evidence was found, how it was handled, and everything that happened to it by documenting:

    1. Discovery: Where, when, and by whom the evidence was collected.
    2. Examination: Where, when, and by whom it was handled or analyzed.
    3. Custody & Storage: Who had possession of it, during what exact period, and how it was secured.
    4. Transfer: When and how custody changed, including tracking numbers or shipping details for external transit.

    By maintaining a flawless chain of custody, digital forensic professionals ensure that the digital artifacts they uncover can be trusted blindly by the courts.
