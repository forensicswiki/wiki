In evidentiary domains such as Digital Forensics and Incident Response (DFIR) and legal
proceedings, analytical outputs must satisfy strict standards of reliability, reproducibility, and
admissibility, such as the *Daubert* standard or Federal Rule of Evidence 702. Whether relying on
legacy manual tools or modern generative AI assistants, introducing flawed data, unverified
inferences, or silent failures can invalidate an investigation or legal filing.

## Traditional (Non-AI) Evidentiary Risks

Traditional tools, such as hex viewers, deterministic parsers, and database viewers, operate on
rigid (deterministic) logic. However, human-in-the-loop analysis introduces distinct cognitive and
procedural vulnerabilities:

* **Confirmation Bias &amp; Tunnel Vision:** Investigators often form an initial hypothesis prematurely
  and selectively gather or interpret artifacts that confirm it while ignoring
  [exculpatory evidence](digital_evidence.md).
* **Parser &amp; Tool Anomalies:** Software bugs, legacy parsing rules, or outdated schema definitions
  can misinterpret raw byte sequences, leading to corrupted timeline reconstructions or missed
  artifacts.
* **Chain-of-Custody Breakage:** Manual extraction, unlogged file conversions, or improper
  time-zone normalization can alter metadata, compromising the legal integrity of primary evidence.
* **Scope and Granularity Collapse:** Human fatigue during manual review of hundreds of terrabytes
  of logs can cause analysts to overlook rare anomalies, edge cases, or sub-second event intervals.

## AI-Assisted &amp; Harness Infrastructure Risks

When generative AI is integrated into analytical workflows via agents, plugins, Model Context
Protocol (MCP) servers, or custom skills (`SKILL.md`), new structural and systemic risks emerge
across the execution harness:

* **Indirect Prompt Injection:** Malicious payloads embedded within parsed evidence (such as
  crafted comments in email headers, script logs, or web archives) can hijack the model's instruction
  context, causing it to bypass safety boundaries or alter its analysis.
* **Opaque Harness Orchestration:** Automated background summarization, sliding context windows,
  and token truncation can silently drop critical log lines or negative evidence without leaving an
  auditable trail.
* **Silent Tool Failures:** If a specialized plugin or database query fails or returns a null set,
  vulnerable LLMs may silently fail to report the error, fabricating plausible data to maintain
  conversational continuity.
* **Automation Bias:** Practitioners are statistically prone to over-relying on structured,
  authoritative-sounding tabular outputs generated by AI tools, reducing their critical skepticism
  during manual verification.

## Dedicated AI/LLM-Specific Risks

Beyond systemic harness failures, the underlying probabilistic architecture of Large Language
Models introduces distinct cognitive and semantic failure modes that directly threaten evidentiary
rigor:

| Risk Category | Mechanism in LLMs | Evidentiary Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Conceptual Conflation** | High-dimensional vector collapse merges distinct domain-specific terms into overlapping semantic neighborhoods. | Blurring strict statutory definitions or confusing technical artifacts (e.g., conflating an attacker's persistence mechanism with routine system uptime). |
| **Hallucination &amp; Fabricated Provenance** | Probabilistic token generation optimizes for syntactic plausibility rather than factual truth. | Generating non-existent Windows Event IDs, fabricated file hashes, or fictitious case law to justify an investigative claim. |
| **Epistemic Overclaiming** | Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) alignment penalizes hesitation and rewards confident, authoritative prose. | Presenting low-confidence correlations or weak circumstantial inferences as definitive, court-ready proof. |
| **Narrative Smoothing** | Autoregressive models bridge gaps by inserting the most statistically probable sequence of events. | Fabricating intermediate lateral movement steps or assuming unrecorded user intent to make sparse log timelines read like a cohesive story. |
| **Context Exhaustion &amp; Compression** | Fixed token limits trigger automated summarization algorithms during long-session analysis. | Truncating raw evidentiary data and dropping critical temporal or anomaly details required for comprehensive reconstruction. |

## Conclusion

While traditional evidentiary risks stem primarily from human oversight and deterministic parser
errors, AI-assisted workflows introduce **persuasive, silent failures**, such as conceptual
conflation and epistemic overclaiming, that pass superficial human review. Maintaining admissibility
and evidentiary integrity requires pairing AI tooling with strict deterministic gates, immutable
audit logs, structured argumentation frameworks (such as Analysis of Competing Hypotheses), and
mandatory manual verification of every generated assertion.
